The problem

A squad is three people and four tools that do not know about each other.

Nothing here is a tooling gap in the abstract. This is where the work actually goes missing.

01

The group chat

Where the good idea was posted at 01:40 and scrolled past by morning. No structure, no history you can search, no way to say who owns it.

02

The shared doc

The proposal, the template library, the one snippet that actually worked. Version three lives in someone's Downloads folder and nobody can find it at 2am.

03

The board nobody opens

Set up with great intentions in week one. Abandoned by week three because finishing a card gave you nothing back.

Campus telemetry

Everyone on campus sits somewhere on the same ladder.

Connect a judge and Squadron reads your rating, then plots the whole cohort on one scale. A squad can see the range it has — and the range it is missing — before it commits to three people for a season.

The workspace

Channels, compiler and board — scoped to the squad, not to you.

Switch squads from the panel at the top and everything changes with it: the channels, the board, the calendar, the standings. One workspace per team, not one workspace per person.

Channels

Discussion that survives the night

Make as many channels as the squad needs and give each one its own symbol. Your messages sit right, everyone else's left, and unread counts tell you where the conversation moved.

  • Any emoji or sign as the channel icon — not just #
  • Hold the microphone to send a voice note instead of typing
  • Code arrives as a real block with a copy button, not a wall of text

Task board

Every card is worth something

Drag a card into Solved and the XP pays out immediately — to you and to the standings. Move it back and the XP is withdrawn. Every task is worth the same, so nobody can price their own work.

  • Drag with a mouse, or pick up and move with the keyboard
  • A flat 5 XP per task, set by the server — not by the filer
  • Assign it, tag it, and see it land on the calendar
